# ICG — Project identity

Created: November 14, 2026 (Asia/Manila).

This is the working identity for the ICG rebuild: a guide for its personality,
writing, and everyday product decisions. It builds on the existing name and
Campus Marquee direction. New language here is a creative proposal, not a claim
of university endorsement or an approved institutional mission.

## Identity at a glance

| Element | Identity |
| --- | --- |
| Name | ICG — Integrated Communications Group |
| Home | Pamantasan ng Lungsod ng Pasig (PLP) |
| Purpose | Help the PLP community follow campus life, plan what comes next, and revisit what happened. |
| Positioning | PLP's news and events record, with the calendar as the main way in. |
| Personality | The campus chronicler: observant, welcoming, dependable, and alive to what matters here. |
| Signature line | **Campus life, on the record.** |
| Visual expression | The Campus Marquee: Evergreen and Marigold, bold dates, real campus photographs, and calm reading spaces. |

**One-sentence introduction**

ICG brings PLP's stories, events, and announcements into one shared campus record,
so you can see what's happening, find the details, and look back.

## The person behind the product

Imagine the person at the campus desk who remembers what is happening this week,
knows where to find the details, and makes room for your story. They notice the
student project as well as the university ceremony. They can write a striking
headline and give a straightforward answer when you need help.

That is ICG's personality. Its confidence comes from specific information. Its
warmth comes from treating readers, contributors, and the people in its stories
with care. Its energy comes from campus life itself.

Students are the primary everyday audience. Faculty, staff, organizers, alumni,
and visitors should also be able to understand a story or plan a visit without
already knowing the campus's acronyms and routines. Writers, reviewers, and
admins need a quieter workspace that helps them do careful work.

## Four traits to make tangible

| Trait | How it shows up | What weakens it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Observant | Name the people, place, date, and detail that make a story specific to PLP. | Generic praise that could describe any university. |
| Welcoming | Use familiar words, explain unfamiliar terms, and give people a clear next step. | Insider shorthand, forced slang, or jokes at a reader's expense. |
| Dependable | Distinguish confirmed details from pending information; acknowledge changes plainly. | Unsupported promises, vague dates, or success messages before an action completes. |
| Lively | Use active verbs, strong photographs, and headlines with something concrete to say. | Clickbait, constant exclamation marks, or treating every update as a celebration. |

## Voice and tone

**The voice stays clear, human, and grounded in campus life.** The tone changes
with the reader's situation.

| Situation | Tone | Example wording |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Home and discovery | Energetic and inviting | “Your week at PLP.” / “See what's coming up.” |
| News and features | Specific and interested | “Students bring their capstone projects to the campus exhibit.” |
| Event details | Practical and direct | “Friday, November 20, 2026 · 2–4 pm · University auditorium.” |
| Announcements | Brief and factual | “The venue has changed. The event will now take place in the university auditorium.” |
| Editorial workspace | Calm and precise | “Draft saved.” / “Send for review.” |
| Empty calendar | Helpful and unhurried | “No events listed for this day. Choose another date.” |
| Failed save | Honest and useful | “We couldn't save your changes. Try again.” |
| Sensitive news | Restrained and considerate | State the verified facts plainly; omit promotional language and celebratory styling. |

These are writing examples, including an illustrative event date and venue, not
live campus information or specifications for features. Use a status message only
when the application can verify the state it describes. Describe recovery or
preserved work only when that behavior is confirmed.

### Writing habits

- Start with what the reader came to find. Put the date, time, location, or required
  action before background explanation when those details drive the decision.
- Use sentence case in copy. Display capitals belong to the Anton treatment
  defined in `DESIGN.md`; they are a visual choice, not a louder writing voice.
- Prefer concrete verbs: “Read the story,” “Open the calendar,” “Save draft,” and
  “Send for review.” Keep familiar navigation labels easy to recognize.
- Use “you” to address the reader. Use “we” when ICG is actually speaking or taking
  responsibility; attribute statements made by other campus offices.
- Write out Integrated Communications Group and Pamantasan ng Lungsod ng Pasig on
  first reference when context is missing. ICG and PLP work in familiar site chrome.
- Use English as the current interface baseline. Preserve people's names and the
  original titles of events and organizations, including Filipino titles; explain
  meaning where needed. A bilingual interface is a separate product decision.
- Give dates enough context to remain useful in the archive. Label event dates
  separately from publication dates. Specify Philippine Time when an audience
  outside the Philippines could otherwise misunderstand an event time.
- Match serious situations with serious language. Errors should explain the problem
  and available action without blame, cute metaphors, or invented reassurance.

## A small language kit

**Signature line:** Campus life, on the record.

Use it in a brand introduction or a suitable footer position. It does not need to
appear on every page or beside every ICG wordmark.

**Short description:** Stories, events, and announcements from the PLP community.

**About introduction:** Campus life moves quickly. ICG brings together the stories,
events, and announcements that help the PLP community stay informed and take part.
The calendar helps you plan ahead; the news and archive help you follow the story.

**The editorial workspace:** “The ICG desk” is a friendly name for the place where
contributors work. Keep Writer, Reviewer, and Admin as the role names. Personality
should not make permissions or publishing states harder to understand.

**Useful recurring phrases:** “This week,” “Coming up,” “Latest news,” “On this day,”
and “Browse the archive.” Their connection to time makes the identity recognizable
without repeatedly using a slogan.

Avoid claims such as “Nothing is ever lost,” “Every story is verified,” “The voice
of every student,” or guaranteed publishing turnaround times. The identity expresses
care and purpose; operational and institutional claims need evidence and approval.

## How the personality looks and behaves

The existing [design system](DESIGN.md) owns all visual tokens and component rules.
This identity explains their character:

- **Evergreen night** gives stories a stage and the record a sense of gravity.
- **Marigold** directs attention to today, the main action, or a selection. Its
  warmth comes from deliberate use.
- **Anton** makes the important date or headline visible at a glance.
- **Public Sans** gives the details room to be understood.
- **Real PLP photography** makes this a record of a particular community. Choose
  images with care, accurate captions, and appropriate permission.
- **The large date numerals** are the signature motif: a recognizable connection
  between what is happening now and what becomes part of the record.
- **Calm lists and workspaces** respect the reader's time. Keep expressive moments
  at the top of public pages; keep editing and review focused on the task.

Retain the established ICG wordmark. A new mascot, logo, palette, or decorative
effect is unnecessary to express this personality. Follow the existing flat
surfaces, contrast, focus, and reduced-motion rules in `DESIGN.md`.

## Use this when making decisions

Before publishing copy or extending an interface, ask:

1. Could this belong specifically to PLP, or is it interchangeable campus marketing?
2. Can someone quickly find what happened, when it happens, and what to do next?
3. Does the language match what is known and what the product actually does?
4. Is the energy appropriate to the subject and the reader's task?
5. Does it feel like the same campus desk across news, calendar, archive, and editing?

Use this file for personality and language, `DESIGN.md` for visual decisions, and
`tasks/contracts.md` plus confirmed decisions in `tasks/decisions.md` for behavior
and permissions. Copy examples do not change those contracts. G16-CMS remains the
